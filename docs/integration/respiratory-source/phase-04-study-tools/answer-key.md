## Multiple Choice (106)

### mc-001: Which function of the respiratory system directly contributes to blood pH control?
**Answer:** Changing the amount of CO₂ exhaled
**Why:** Ventilation controls CO₂, which is linked by equilibrium to H⁺ and bicarbonate.

### mc-002: Which structure belongs to the upper respiratory system as defined in these lectures?
**Answer:** Nasopharynx
**Why:** The course groups the nose, sinuses, nasal cavity, and pharynx as upper respiratory structures; larynx and lower airways are lower.

### mc-003: Which part of the respiratory tract is normally devoted to conduction rather than direct gas exchange?
**Answer:** Terminal bronchioles
**Why:** Terminal bronchioles end the conducting portion; the first alveoli appear at respiratory bronchioles.

### mc-004: How does pulmonary ventilation differ from gas diffusion?
**Answer:** Ventilation moves air in bulk; diffusion moves individual gas molecules down partial-pressure gradients
**Why:** Pressure-driven bulk flow moves air, while microscopic gradients drive alveolar and tissue exchange.

### mc-005: Which activity is an additional respiratory function rather than a step of oxygen delivery?
**Answer:** Sound production using vocal folds
**Why:** The larynx generates voiced sound using exhaled air.

### mc-006: Which sequence correctly describes the basic route of inspired air?
**Answer:** Nose or mouth → pharynx → larynx → trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli
**Why:** Air passes from the pharynx through the larynx and trachea before branching through the bronchial tree.

### mc-007: Which statement distinguishes internal respiration from cellular respiration most accurately?
**Answer:** Internal respiration concerns gas exchange with tissues; cellular respiration concerns metabolism that uses O₂ to produce ATP
**Why:** The terms name different processes; the lecture sometimes presents both together for teaching.

### mc-008: Why do nasal conchae help condition inspired air?
**Answer:** They increase mucosal contact and airflow mixing
**Why:** Conchae create convoluted pathways, improving contact with warm, moist mucosa.

### mc-009: What are the choanae?
**Answer:** Posterior openings from the nasal cavity into the nasopharynx
**Why:** The internal nares/choanae connect the nasal cavity with the nasopharynx.

### mc-010: Which structure is made chiefly of bone and separates the front of the mouth from the nasal cavity?
**Answer:** Hard palate
**Why:** The hard palate forms the bony anterior partition; the soft palate is muscular posteriorly.

### mc-011: What distinguishes the nasopharynx from the lower two pharyngeal subdivisions?
**Answer:** It primarily conveys air and is behind the nasal cavity
**Why:** The nasopharynx is superior, above the soft palate and behind the nasal passages.

### mc-012: What is the main role of vibrissae?
**Answer:** Trap relatively large particles near the nostrils
**Why:** Nasal hairs filter larger material at the nasal vestibule.

### mc-013: What connects the nasopharynx to the middle ear for pressure equalization?
**Answer:** Pharyngotympanic tube
**Why:** The auditory/Eustachian tube runs between middle ear and nasopharynx.

### mc-014: Which observation is a defensible benefit of nasal compared with mouth breathing?
**Answer:** Incoming air is warmed, humidified, and filtered by the nasal passages
**Why:** Nasal airway conditioning prepares the air for delicate lower airway surfaces.

### mc-015: Which pairing best describes respiratory mucosa?
**Answer:** Epithelium above an underlying lamina propria
**Why:** The conducting airway mucosa consists of a lining epithelium and supporting loose connective tissue.

### mc-016: What is the primary direction of mucus movement in the mucociliary escalator?
**Answer:** Toward the pharynx
**Why:** Coordinated ciliary beating transports trapped debris upward for swallowing or expulsion.

### mc-017: Why does the epithelial lining become very thin at the alveoli?
**Answer:** Minimize the diffusion distance for gas exchange
**Why:** Simple squamous type I cells create a thin gas-exchange barrier.

### mc-018: Which epithelial type is characteristic of much of the nasal cavity and larger conducting airways?
**Answer:** Pseudostratified ciliated columnar epithelium
**Why:** Conducting airways often use ciliated, mucus-producing pseudostratified epithelium.

### mc-019: Which epithelial type is especially suited to abrasion in the oropharynx?
**Answer:** Stratified squamous epithelium
**Why:** Multiple layers offer protection at shared food/air passages.

### mc-020: A person whose airway cilia are severely impaired would most directly struggle with what?
**Answer:** Removing mucus and trapped particles from conducting passages
**Why:** Ciliary transport is a key airway clearance mechanism.

### mc-021: Which airway is more commonly lined by simple cuboidal epithelium rather than cartilage-supported pseudostratified epithelium?
**Answer:** Bronchiole
**Why:** The epithelial pattern typically shifts toward simple cuboidal epithelium in small bronchioles.

### mc-022: Which group lists only the lecture's unpaired laryngeal cartilages?
**Answer:** Epiglottis, thyroid, cricoid
**Why:** Epiglottis, thyroid, and cricoid are unpaired; arytenoid, corniculate, and cuneiform occur in pairs.

### mc-023: What most directly produces voiced sound?
**Answer:** Vibrations of the true vocal folds as air passes through the glottis
**Why:** Phonation requires vocal-fold vibration controlled by laryngeal muscles.

### mc-024: What is the principal task of the intrinsic laryngeal muscles?
**Answer:** Adjust vocal-fold position and tension
**Why:** Intrinsic muscles move the vocal apparatus; extrinsic muscles mainly position the larynx.

### mc-025: How does increasing vocal-fold tension generally affect fundamental pitch, with other factors held constant?
**Answer:** It raises pitch
**Why:** Tighter folds usually vibrate at higher frequency; mass and length also influence pitch.

### mc-026: What is the epiglottis's defensive role?
**Answer:** Help divert swallowed material away from the laryngeal inlet
**Why:** It participates with laryngeal elevation and fold closure in protecting the airway.

### mc-027: Which sequence captures the forceful cough mechanism?
**Answer:** Close glottis, build intrathoracic pressure, suddenly open it to expel air
**Why:** Compression against a closed glottis followed by sudden opening yields rapid expiratory flow.

### mc-028: What are the vestibular folds?
**Answer:** False vocal cords located superior to true vocal folds
**Why:** Vestibular folds contribute to protection, not the main mechanism of voiced sound.

### mc-029: What is the specific contribution of the arytenoid cartilages?
**Answer:** Move the vocal folds to adjust the glottic opening
**Why:** Paired arytenoids act as movable anchors for vocal-fold positioning.

### mc-030: Why are tracheal cartilage rings C-shaped rather than fully circular?
**Answer:** They maintain patency while allowing the esophagus behind the trachea to expand
**Why:** A posterior gap allows flexibility and accommodation for swallowing.

### mc-031: Where is the trachealis muscle found?
**Answer:** Across the posterior opening of the C-shaped tracheal cartilages
**Why:** This smooth muscle spans the posterior tracheal wall and can alter its diameter.

### mc-032: Which landmark marks the division into the two primary bronchi?
**Answer:** Carina
**Why:** The carina is the internal ridge at the tracheal bifurcation.

### mc-033: Why are aspirated solid objects more likely to enter the right main bronchus?
**Answer:** It is generally wider, shorter, and more vertical
**Why:** The right main bronchus is anatomically more aligned with the tracheal axis.

### mc-034: Which structures supply individual lobes of the lungs?
**Answer:** Secondary bronchi
**Why:** Lobar/secondary bronchi branch into each lung lobe; tertiary bronchi serve segments.

### mc-035: How do bronchioles differ from bronchi?
**Answer:** Bronchioles lack cartilage and have smooth muscle regulating their diameter
**Why:** Bronchioles rely heavily on smooth muscle rather than cartilaginous support.

### mc-036: Which autonomic signal is associated with bronchodilation in the lecture?
**Answer:** Sympathetic activation, especially β₂ adrenergic signaling
**Why:** Adrenergic relaxation tends to increase bronchiolar radius, lowering resistance.

### mc-037: Why do terminal bronchioles differ from respiratory bronchioles?
**Answer:** Terminal bronchioles conduct air without alveoli; respiratory bronchioles start bearing alveoli
**Why:** The respiratory zone begins as alveoli appear along respiratory bronchioles.

### mc-038: What is the main difference between the right and left lung lobation?
**Answer:** Right has three lobes and left has two
**Why:** The right has superior, middle, inferior; the left has superior and inferior.

### mc-039: Which fissure is found only in the right lung in typical anatomy?
**Answer:** Horizontal fissure
**Why:** The right lung has horizontal and oblique fissures, while the left normally has an oblique fissure.

### mc-040: The cardiac notch is associated with which lung?
**Answer:** Left lung
**Why:** The heart indents the anterior left lung.

### mc-041: Which pleural layer directly adheres to the lung surface?
**Answer:** Visceral pleura
**Why:** Visceral pleura covers lung; parietal pleura lines chest wall and diaphragm.

### mc-042: What is normally inside the pleural cavity?
**Answer:** A very thin film of lubricating serous fluid
**Why:** The pleural cavity is a potential space containing a thin film of fluid.

### mc-043: What crosses the lung hilum?
**Answer:** Bronchi, blood vessels, lymphatics, and nerves
**Why:** The hilum forms the portal for the root structures of each lung.

### mc-044: Which airway supplies a bronchopulmonary segment?
**Answer:** Tertiary (segmental) bronchus
**Why:** Segmental bronchi define bronchopulmonary segments.

### mc-045: Which alveolar cells form most of the thin gas-exchange surface?
**Answer:** Type I pneumocytes
**Why:** Type I pneumocytes spread thinly across most alveolar surface area.

### mc-046: What is the main function of type II pneumocytes?
**Answer:** Produce surfactant and help replace alveolar epithelial cells
**Why:** Type II cells produce the lipid-protein mixture that stabilizes alveoli.

### mc-047: Why is pulmonary surfactant essential?
**Answer:** It reduces surface tension and limits alveolar collapse
**Why:** Lowering the tension of alveolar lining fluid keeps small air spaces more stable.

### mc-048: What do alveolar macrophages primarily do?
**Answer:** Engulf inhaled debris and pathogens in air spaces
**Why:** Dust cells provide local phagocytic defense in alveoli.

### mc-049: Which layers represent the key respiratory membrane components in the lecture?
**Answer:** Type I alveolar epithelium, fused basal laminae, and capillary endothelium
**Why:** Diffusion crosses an unusually thin epithelial/basal-lamina/endothelial barrier.

### mc-050: Which property would most directly slow gas exchange across the respiratory membrane?
**Answer:** Increased thickness of the exchange barrier
**Why:** Greater distance obstructs diffusion; thinner and larger surfaces facilitate it.

### mc-051: Where are alveolar ducts situated?
**Answer:** After respiratory bronchioles, leading toward clusters of alveoli
**Why:** Alveolar ducts are distal air passages whose walls are largely alveolar openings.

### mc-052: According to Boyle's law, what happens to gas pressure if a fixed quantity of gas expands at constant temperature?
**Answer:** Pressure decreases
**Why:** At constant T, P and V vary inversely (P₁V₁ = P₂V₂).

### mc-053: What initiates normal inhalation?
**Answer:** Diaphragm and external intercostals contract, thoracic volume rises, alveolar pressure falls
**Why:** Expanding the thoracic cavity lowers alveolar pressure relative to outside air.

### mc-054: Why is quiet expiration usually passive?
**Answer:** Elastic recoil decreases lung volume when inspiratory muscles relax
**Why:** Recoil makes alveolar pressure transiently exceed atmosphere, causing outward flow.

### mc-055: When alveolar pressure is 759 mmHg and atmospheric pressure is 760 mmHg, what happens?
**Answer:** Air moves into the lungs
**Why:** Air flows down the pressure gradient, from atmospheric to the slightly lower alveolar pressure.

### mc-056: Which muscles are the professor's two most important muscles of inspiration?
**Answer:** Diaphragm and external intercostals
**Why:** These muscles expand the thoracic cavity during normal inspiration.

### mc-057: How does forced expiration differ from quiet expiration?
**Answer:** It actively recruits expiratory muscles such as abdominal muscles and internal intercostals
**Why:** Forced expiration uses additional muscle work rather than simple recoil alone.

### mc-058: If bronchiolar radius decreases, what happens to airflow resistance, all else equal?
**Answer:** Resistance increases markedly
**Why:** Small airways can strongly modulate resistance with smooth-muscle-induced diameter changes.

### mc-059: What is tidal volume?
**Answer:** Air moved in or out during an ordinary resting breath
**Why:** TV or VT describes the volume of an ordinary breath.

### mc-060: What is residual volume?
**Answer:** Air remaining after maximal forced expiration
**Why:** RV remains even after a maximal breath out, preventing total lung emptying.

### mc-061: Which formula gives vital capacity?
**Answer:** TV + IRV + ERV
**Why:** Vital capacity is maximum exhalable volume after full inhalation, excluding RV.

### mc-062: Which formula gives total lung capacity?
**Answer:** VC + RV
**Why:** TLC includes all air in the lungs at maximal inflation, including residual air.

### mc-063: What does the inspiratory reserve volume measure?
**Answer:** Additional air inhalable above a normal tidal inspiration
**Why:** IRV is the extra inhalation possible after an ordinary breath in.

### mc-064: Which quantity cannot be measured directly with ordinary spirometry alone?
**Answer:** Residual volume
**Why:** Standard spirometry measures moved air and cannot directly capture air that stays after maximal expiration.

### mc-065: If ERV and RV are added together, what capacity results?
**Answer:** Functional residual capacity
**Why:** FRC is the lung volume remaining after a normal passive expiration.

### mc-066: What is a gas's partial pressure?
**Answer:** Its individual contribution to the total pressure of a gas mixture
**Why:** PO₂ and PCO₂ each reflect a component gas's pressure contribution.

### mc-067: What does Henry's law describe in respiratory physiology?
**Answer:** The dissolved concentration of a gas is proportional to its partial pressure and solubility at constant temperature
**Why:** Henry's law relates dissolved gas to pressure and its solubility properties.

### mc-068: Why does O₂ diffuse from alveoli into pulmonary blood?
**Answer:** Alveolar PO₂ is typically higher than that of incoming deoxygenated pulmonary blood
**Why:** Net diffusion follows the oxygen partial-pressure gradient across the exchange membrane.

### mc-069: Why does CO₂ diffuse from pulmonary capillaries toward alveolar air?
**Answer:** Incoming blood PCO₂ exceeds alveolar PCO₂
**Why:** CO₂ moves down a partial-pressure gradient from blood to alveoli.

### mc-070: Which pairing is correct for internal (systemic) gas exchange?
**Answer:** O₂ from blood to tissues; CO₂ from tissues to blood
**Why:** Consuming tissues lower their PO₂ and generate CO₂, establishing complementary gradients.

### mc-071: If thick fluid or inflammation increases the alveolar diffusion distance, what is the expected effect?
**Answer:** Gas exchange may become less efficient
**Why:** Exchange depends on the thickness, area, and gradients across the membrane.

### mc-072: What is oxyhemoglobin?
**Answer:** Hemoglobin with oxygen bound to heme groups
**Why:** HbO₂ names oxygen-carrying hemoglobin.

### mc-073: Why does the oxygen–hemoglobin saturation curve have a sigmoidal shape?
**Answer:** Cooperative binding changes hemoglobin's affinity as O₂ binds
**Why:** The hemoglobin tetramer's binding sites influence one another.

### mc-074: What favors O₂ unloading in active, CO₂-producing tissues?
**Answer:** Increased CO₂ and H⁺ lower hemoglobin oxygen affinity
**Why:** The Bohr effect helps supply active tissues under more acidic, CO₂-rich conditions.

### mc-075: At high alveolar PO₂, what is expected of normal hemoglobin?
**Answer:** A high fraction of available O₂-binding sites becomes occupied
**Why:** High PO₂ favors hemoglobin oxygen loading on the flat upper part of the saturation curve.

### mc-076: Which explanation best connects tissue metabolism and hemoglobin behavior?
**Answer:** Working tissues consume O₂ and produce CO₂/H⁺, favoring diffusion and hemoglobin unloading
**Why:** Lower tissue PO₂ plus changes in pH and CO₂ help direct oxygen where needed.

### mc-077: How does oxygen dissolved in plasma differ from hemoglobin-bound oxygen?
**Answer:** Dissolved O₂ directly determines PO₂ but is a small share of total O₂ content
**Why:** Dissolved gas contributes to partial pressure; most oxygen content travels bound to Hb.

### mc-078: In what form is most CO₂-derived carbon carried in blood?
**Answer:** Bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻)
**Why:** The lecture's ~70% pathway predominantly yields bicarbonate, after transient carbonic acid formation.

### mc-079: What enzyme accelerates the reversible hydration of CO₂ in red blood cells?
**Answer:** Carbonic anhydrase
**Why:** Carbonic anhydrase rapidly links dissolved CO₂ with the H₂CO₃/HCO₃⁻ equilibrium.

### mc-080: What is the chloride shift?
**Answer:** HCO₃⁻ exchanges with Cl⁻ across the red-cell membrane during CO₂ transport
**Why:** Bicarbonate exits RBCs in systemic tissues as chloride enters, with a reverse exchange at the lungs.

### mc-081: What happens to blood pH if CO₂ accumulates because ventilation is inadequate?
**Answer:** It tends to fall as CO₂ drives H⁺ generation
**Why:** The CO₂/bicarbonate reaction can increase H⁺ and lead to respiratory acidosis.

### mc-082: Why can hyperventilation increase blood pH?
**Answer:** Excess CO₂ removal shifts the buffer equilibrium toward lower H⁺
**Why:** Reducing arterial PCO₂ decreases carbonic acid-related acidity, potentially causing respiratory alkalosis.

### mc-083: How does CO₂ bind in carbaminohemoglobin?
**Answer:** To amino groups on globin protein chains
**Why:** Carbaminohemoglobin contains CO₂ chemically attached to globin, not to the heme oxygen site.

### mc-084: Which approximate division matches the lecture's CO₂ transport percentages?
**Answer:** 70% bicarbonate pathway, 23% carbamino compounds, 7% dissolved
**Why:** These values are teaching approximations: predominantly bicarbonate, then protein-bound, then dissolved gas.

### mc-085: What brainstem region contains networks essential for breathing rhythm?
**Answer:** Medulla oblongata
**Why:** The medulla houses interacting neuronal groups needed for automatic respiration.

### mc-086: Which choice best reflects the lecture's DRG versus VRG distinction?
**Answer:** DRG emphasizes inspiratory control, while VRG is more engaged when respiratory demand increases
**Why:** This is the instructor's functional shorthand; modern medullary networks are more interconnected.

### mc-087: What is the main role of the pons in breathing?
**Answer:** Modulate the timing and pattern of medullary respiratory activity
**Why:** Pontine centers shape transitions between inspiration and expiration.

### mc-088: In the older nomenclature used by the course, which center tends to facilitate sustained inspiration?
**Answer:** Apneustic center
**Why:** Apneustic is a historical functional label for pontine inspiratory-facilitating mechanisms.

### mc-089: Which center in the older lecture naming helps limit or terminate inspiration?
**Answer:** Pneumotaxic center
**Why:** The pneumotaxic label denotes inspiratory-limiting pontine influence.

### mc-090: Why do central chemoreceptors respond strongly to a rise in arterial CO₂?
**Answer:** CO₂ alters brain extracellular/CSF pH, affecting chemosensitive neurons
**Why:** CO₂ can influence CSF acidity, which central respiratory networks detect.

### mc-091: Which receptors are particularly important when arterial PO₂ falls markedly?
**Answer:** Peripheral carotid-body chemoreceptors
**Why:** Peripheral chemoreceptors contribute significantly to the response to reduced arterial O₂.

### mc-092: What nerve directly carries the primary motor drive to the diaphragm?
**Answer:** Phrenic nerve
**Why:** Motor neurons from C3–C5 form the phrenic nerve innervating the diaphragm.

### mc-093: Which term describes inadequate oxygen availability in tissues?
**Answer:** Hypoxia
**Why:** Hypoxia refers to tissue oxygen deficiency; arterial and tissue oxygenation are related but not identical.

### mc-094: Why is acute epiglottitis potentially life-threatening?
**Answer:** Swelling can obstruct the laryngeal inlet and airway
**Why:** Inflammation in this small upper-airway region can reduce airflow sharply.

### mc-095: Why does pneumonia frequently impair gas exchange?
**Answer:** Inflammation and fluid in alveolar regions increase diffusion distance and impair ventilation
**Why:** Alveolar exudate, collapse, and impaired ventilation/perfusion interfere with exchange.

### mc-096: What is a central airway problem in cystic fibrosis?
**Answer:** Thick, poorly cleared mucus promotes blockage and infection
**Why:** CFTR dysfunction disrupts airway surface hydration and mucociliary clearance.

### mc-097: What directly increases airflow resistance during many asthma episodes?
**Answer:** Bronchial smooth-muscle constriction plus airway inflammation
**Why:** Narrowed, inflamed airways resist airflow and cause variable obstruction.

### mc-098: Why can laryngitis make the voice hoarse?
**Answer:** Inflamed vocal folds vibrate abnormally
**Why:** Swelling affects normal vocal-fold vibration and sound generation.

### mc-099: A gas moving across the alveolar wall toward blood is an example of what process?
**Answer:** Diffusion rather than bulk ventilation
**Why:** Molecules cross the respiratory membrane down partial-pressure gradients; air movement in tubes is ventilation.

### mc-100: During swallowing, which structure helps close access to the nasopharynx?
**Answer:** Soft palate
**Why:** The soft palate elevates, helping keep swallowed material out of the nasal passage.

### mc-101: Which paired cartilages sit atop the arytenoid cartilages?
**Answer:** Corniculate
**Why:** Corniculate cartilages are small horns attached to the arytenoids.

### mc-102: Why does intrapleural pressure matter for ventilation?
**Answer:** It helps keep lung surfaces mechanically coupled to the chest wall through the pleura
**Why:** The pleural fluid interface and normally subatmospheric pressure allow chest expansion to expand lungs.

### mc-103: Which formula gives inspiratory capacity?
**Answer:** TV + IRV
**Why:** Inspiratory capacity combines a normal tidal inspiration and the extra inspiratory reserve after ordinary expiration.

### mc-104: At the same PO₂, which change would generally shift the Hb–O₂ dissociation curve to the right?
**Answer:** Higher CO₂ and lower pH
**Why:** Increased CO₂/H⁺ decreases affinity, facilitating O₂ unloading; this is the Bohr effect.

### mc-105: Where does the bicarbonate-to-CO₂ reaction reverse to help eliminate CO₂?
**Answer:** In pulmonary capillary blood as CO₂ diffuses into alveoli
**Why:** Reversible chemistry in blood regenerates diffusible CO₂ in the lungs.

### mc-106: Why can low arterial PO₂ stimulate breathing even though central receptors respond strongly to CO₂?
**Answer:** Peripheral chemoreceptors sense the O₂ decrease and signal brainstem circuits
**Why:** Carotid and aortic bodies provide oxygen-sensitive input complementary to central CO₂/pH sensing.

## Fill in the Blank (67)

### fib-001: The bulk movement of air into and out of lungs is called _____.
**Answer:** pulmonary ventilation
**Accepted alternatives:** breathing, ventilation
**Hint:** Think about moving air, not exchanging molecules across a membrane.
**Why:** Ventilation depends on pressure gradients created by thoracic muscle activity.

### fib-002: The respiratory system contributes to blood _____ by regulating CO₂ exhalation.
**Answer:** pH
**Accepted alternatives:** acid-base balance, acid-base regulation
**Hint:** Consider the concentration of hydrogen ions.
**Why:** CO₂ shifts the CO₂/HCO₃⁻ equilibrium and can change acidity.

### fib-003: The region ending at terminal bronchioles is the _____ portion of the respiratory tract.
**Answer:** conducting
**Accepted alternatives:** conducting zone
**Hint:** No direct alveolar exchange occurs in this portion.
**Why:** The conducting zone transports and conditions air; alveoli appear farther downstream.

### fib-004: The exchange of gases between systemic capillaries and tissue cells is _____ respiration.
**Answer:** internal
**Accepted alternatives:** None
**Hint:** This occurs away from the lungs in body tissues.
**Why:** Internal respiration refers to systemic gas exchange, distinct from cellular ATP production.

### fib-005: The posterior openings of the nasal cavities into the nasopharynx are the _____.
**Answer:** choanae
**Accepted alternatives:** internal nares
**Hint:** They are sometimes called the internal nostrils.
**Why:** Choanae connect the nasal cavity and nasopharynx.

### fib-006: The curved folds that increase airflow turbulence in the nose are nasal _____.
**Answer:** conchae
**Accepted alternatives:** turbinates
**Hint:** There are superior, middle, and inferior ones.
**Why:** Conchae increase airflow contact with respiratory mucosa.

### fib-007: The passageway immediately below a nasal concha is a nasal _____.
**Answer:** meatus
**Accepted alternatives:** None
**Hint:** A three-part series of air passages accompanies the conchae.
**Why:** A meatus is the airway space beneath a concha.

### fib-008: The nasal hair structures that intercept larger airborne particles are _____.
**Answer:** vibrissae
**Accepted alternatives:** nasal hairs, nose hairs
**Hint:** They are coarse hairs near the entrance to the nose.
**Why:** Vibrissae help trap larger particulates before they reach smaller passages.

### fib-009: The upper pharyngeal region lying behind the nasal cavity is the _____.
**Answer:** nasopharynx
**Accepted alternatives:** None
**Hint:** It begins above the soft palate.
**Why:** The nasopharynx primarily conducts air.

### fib-010: The beating cilia that move mucus toward the pharynx are part of the mucociliary _____.
**Answer:** escalator
**Accepted alternatives:** clearance system
**Hint:** Also called the mucus escalator.
**Why:** Ciliary transport removes trapped particles and microbes.

### fib-011: The connective-tissue layer beneath the airway epithelium is the _____.
**Answer:** lamina propria
**Accepted alternatives:** None
**Hint:** It supports epithelial cells, vessels, and immune components.
**Why:** The lamina propria is part of the mucosa.

### fib-012: Much of the trachea is lined by _____ ciliated columnar epithelium.
**Answer:** pseudostratified
**Accepted alternatives:** None
**Hint:** Nuclei appear at different heights despite cells contacting the basement membrane.
**Why:** This specialized epithelium often includes goblet cells.

### fib-013: The one-cell-thick, flattened epithelial lining most suited for alveolar diffusion is simple _____.
**Answer:** squamous
**Accepted alternatives:** None
**Hint:** Thin flat cells minimize diffusion distance.
**Why:** Type I pneumocytes are squamous cells.

### fib-014: The mucus-producing epithelial cells common in larger conducting airways are _____ cells.
**Answer:** goblet
**Accepted alternatives:** None
**Hint:** Named after their cup-like shape.
**Why:** Goblet cells secrete airway mucus.

### fib-015: The true vocal cords are the vocal _____.
**Answer:** folds
**Accepted alternatives:** None
**Hint:** Contrast them with the false vocal cords.
**Why:** True vocal folds vibrate during phonation.

### fib-016: The false vocal cords are the _____ folds.
**Answer:** vestibular
**Accepted alternatives:** None
**Hint:** These folds lie superior to the true folds.
**Why:** Vestibular folds contribute to airway protection.

### fib-017: The unpaired laryngeal cartilage forming a complete ring is the _____.
**Answer:** cricoid
**Accepted alternatives:** cricoid cartilage
**Hint:** It lies inferior to the thyroid cartilage.
**Why:** Cricoid forms a ring just above the first tracheal ring.

### fib-018: Production of voice by vibrating the vocal folds is called _____.
**Answer:** phonation
**Accepted alternatives:** None
**Hint:** Not the same as shaping speech with lips and tongue.
**Why:** Phonation is vocal sound production; articulation shapes it into speech.

### fib-019: The paired cartilages that directly adjust vocal-fold positions are the _____.
**Answer:** arytenoids
**Accepted alternatives:** arytenoid cartilages, arytenoid cartilage
**Hint:** These move like small levers at the posterior glottis.
**Why:** Arytenoid motion regulates the opening and closing of the glottis.

### fib-020: The posterior smooth muscle spanning the C-shaped tracheal cartilage rings is the _____.
**Answer:** trachealis
**Accepted alternatives:** trachealis muscle
**Hint:** Its name resembles the airway in which it sits.
**Why:** This muscle changes tracheal diameter and accommodates posterior flexibility.

### fib-021: The tracheal bifurcation contains a prominent internal ridge called the _____.
**Answer:** carina
**Accepted alternatives:** None
**Hint:** Often triggers a cough when contacted.
**Why:** Carina marks division of the main bronchi.

### fib-022: A bronchus delivering air to a single lung lobe is a _____ bronchus.
**Answer:** secondary
**Accepted alternatives:** lobar
**Hint:** Think of the airway hierarchy primary → secondary → tertiary.
**Why:** Secondary/lobar bronchi supply lung lobes.

### fib-023: The last conducting airways before alveoli begin to appear are _____ bronchioles.
**Answer:** terminal
**Accepted alternatives:** None
**Hint:** Their successors are respiratory bronchioles.
**Why:** Terminal bronchioles end the conducting zone.

### fib-024: Unlike bronchi, bronchioles contain no supporting _____.
**Answer:** cartilage
**Accepted alternatives:** cartilaginous plates
**Hint:** Smooth muscle becomes important for airway diameter.
**Why:** Bronchioles lack cartilage, unlike the larger bronchi.

### fib-025: The typical right lung contains _____ lobes.
**Answer:** three
**Accepted alternatives:** 3
**Hint:** More than the left lung.
**Why:** The right lung has superior, middle, and inferior lobes.

### fib-026: The usual left lung is separated by the _____ fissure.
**Answer:** oblique
**Accepted alternatives:** None
**Hint:** Both lungs have this fissure, but only the right typically has a horizontal one.
**Why:** The left lung typically has superior and inferior lobes divided by an oblique fissure.

### fib-027: The indentation in the left lung accommodating the heart is the cardiac _____.
**Answer:** notch
**Accepted alternatives:** None
**Hint:** Found on the anterior border of the left lung.
**Why:** The cardiac notch makes room for the heart.

### fib-028: The serous membrane covering lung tissue directly is the _____ pleura.
**Answer:** visceral
**Accepted alternatives:** None
**Hint:** The opposite layer lines the thoracic wall.
**Why:** Visceral pleura is continuous with parietal pleura at the lung root.

### fib-029: The region of entry for vessels, nerves, and bronchi on the medial lung is the _____.
**Answer:** hilum
**Accepted alternatives:** hilus
**Hint:** It is the lung's root gateway.
**Why:** The lung hilum conveys bronchi and vascular/nerve structures.

### fib-030: The alveolar epithelial cells covering most of the respiratory surface are type _____ pneumocytes.
**Answer:** I
**Accepted alternatives:** 1, type 1, type I
**Hint:** The very thin gas-diffusion cells.
**Why:** Type I cells form the main simple squamous surface.

### fib-031: Surfactant is produced by type _____ pneumocytes.
**Answer:** II
**Accepted alternatives:** 2, type 2, type II
**Hint:** These are also called septal cells.

### fib-032: The mixture lowering alveolar fluid surface tension is pulmonary _____.

### fib-033: The phagocytic dust cells in the alveoli are alveolar _____.

### fib-034: The thin barrier composed of alveolar epithelium, basal laminae, and capillary endothelium is the respiratory _____.

### fib-035: The inverse pressure–volume relation at constant gas temperature is _____ law.

### fib-036: During inhalation the diaphragm _____, flattening and descending.

### fib-037: Normal quiet exhalation mainly uses passive elastic _____.

### fib-038: The primary rib-elevating inspiratory muscles are the _____ intercostals.

### fib-039: If lung pressure is _____ than atmospheric pressure, air flows into the lungs.

### fib-040: The resting air volume entering or leaving with a typical breath is _____ volume.
