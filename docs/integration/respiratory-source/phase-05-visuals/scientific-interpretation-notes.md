# Respiratory gallery — Scientific interpretation and provenance notes

These are **slide-derived illustrations**. Their educational captions describe what the figure depicts; they do not replace careful scientific exposition in the Chapter 23 readings.

- **p10, Boyle's law:** The relationship is `P ∝ 1/V` for a fixed quantity of gas at constant temperature, not the dimensionally literal equality `P = 1/V`. Clinical lungs are not rigid closed gas containers; pressure gradients explain airflow.
- **p11, inhalation/exhalation:** Lecture pressure examples such as 759 versus 761 mmHg illustrate a small pressure difference around atmospheric pressure, not invariant values. Quiet expiration is predominantly passive recoil.
- **p13, spirometry:** Numerical lung-volume values displayed are illustrative and vary by body size, age, sex and physiological condition. Standard spirometry does not directly measure residual volume.
- **p14, Henry's law:** Gas dissolves according to partial pressure and solubility; the soda-can analogy illustrates equilibrium and not every physiological detail of exchange across the respiratory membrane.
- **p15, diffusion gradients:** Partial pressures in the diagrams are representative values and can vary across persons, tissue regions and conditions.
- **p16, oxygen dissociation:** The curve illustrates cooperativity; its right/left shifts also depend on pH, temperature, 2,3-BPG and CO₂, even if not fully depicted in the slide.
- **p17 and p18, carbon dioxide transport:** Most CO₂ reaches plasma predominantly as **bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻)** after carbonic anhydrase catalyzes its conversion inside erythrocytes. The slide's shorthand '70% carbonic acid' does not mean 70% travels as undissociated carbonic acid. The 70/23/7 percentages are approximations.
- **p19, respiratory centers:** 'Pneumotaxic' and 'apneustic' are older textbook labels, not a complete account of modern respiratory neural circuitry; brainstem rhythms also involve medullary networks including the pre-Bötzinger complex. The DRG/VRG roles shown are simplified.
- **Blue blood in lecture graphics:** Blue is a symbolic depiction of lower oxygenation, not the literal appearance of venous blood.
